# Living Signal Workshop — H.4B static art review

This document records the original H.4B art proposal, preserved at commit `4fcedd7`. H.4B.1 supersedes its ambiguous selected still with six clarity-review states documented in `CLARITY_REVIEW.md`. All artwork remains outside `public/` and every Vite entry; the rendered PNGs are ignored local review captures.

## Composition

- Brandon Hann remains the first and largest reading anchor. A skewed field and heavy cutout BUILD form place the independent project routes within one visual language, rather than four dashboard cards. Blue identifies Brandon's authored system; mint, amber, lavender, and blue identify project branches. Color is redundant with project names and roles.
- The relay is a small courier with a split blue cap, mint eye line, messenger strap, warm packet bag, and weighted boots. The sheet tests a three-quarter view, a separately drawn side silhouette, a carrying pose, and a denied response. The 64 px row checks whether its cap, bag, and face direction remain legible at plausible scene scale. These poses are concept drawings, not a finished animation rig.
- SecureAgent alone receives a larger boundary station. In the selected still, the packet reaches a policy aperture and the drawing branches to **ALLOW** and **DENY**. This illustrates the public-safe concept of deterministic authorization before simulated execution. It does not depict a running LLM, external tool action, or a live connection among portfolio projects.

## One possible interaction, after art approval

1. A visitor focuses or selects SecureAgent. Its lavender branch becomes stronger; the other routes remain visible.
2. The relay carries a proposed packet along that same path and stops at the boundary. The station presents an illustrative policy decision before any simulated action.
3. A short, deterministic allow or deny result appears near the station, with a semantic HTML summary and direct case-study link. The relay returns to rest. Escape or a clear control cancels the sequence.

Pointer, touch, and keyboard would operate the same semantic control. Reduced motion would immediately show the selected route, station result, and explanatory text. Motion must be finite, cancelable, offscreen-aware, and absent while idle. No animation is implemented in this checkpoint.

## Art review and known limitations

The static frame is more character-led and asymmetric than H.4A, and desktop routes are physically continuous after the separate H.4A geometry correction. The SecureAgent object has a recognizable purpose, but its exact allow/deny gesture remains illustrative. The relay's hand and facial expression need a further character-design pass before animation; at 64 px, subtle expression details disappear. The mobile keyframe simplifies tabs and keeps all routes within the 390 px canvas, but the station's policy branching is not yet a mobile interaction design. The artwork still uses technical diagram language and should be judged by the owner as a visual proposal, not described as polished or approved.

Estimated next slice, **only if approved**: one finite SecureAgent route and policy sequence, roughly 2–4 focused engineering days plus character motion/art iteration. The main uncertainties are legible 64 px character animation, mobile framing of the allow/deny branches, and accessible synchronization with semantic project content. A dedicated visual review should precede implementation.
